How to play Tete 21

  1. Cards. 36-card deck: Ace, 3–10 in four suits. No King, Queen, Jack or 2. Ace is always 11. Two decks when 7 or 8 play.
  2. Wager. Every seated player puts the agreed wager into the pot before the deal.
  3. Deal. Each player gets 3 cards, one at a time.
  4. Flush. The dealer then opens players + 1 cards face up on the board, in order, so everyone can see them before dropping.
  5. Drop. Starting left of the dealer button, each player drops one card face up. The first card sets the lead suit. If you hold that suit you must drop it; otherwise drop anything. The dropped card leaves your hand.
  6. Trick. Highest card of the lead suit wins (Ace > 10 > 9 … > 3). The winner picks first.
  7. Pick. On your turn you take from the top of the board, one card at a time, as many as you like. You can't pass 21: if the next card would bust you the game stops you and you stay where you are. Then the next player clockwise picks.
  8. Reveal. Closest to 21 wins the pot.
  9. Tie. Agreed when the table is set up: either the tied players each top up another wager and play off (winner takes everything), or they split the pot.
  10. Adasi. Two Aces in your dealt hand wins the pot outright. When the drop reaches you, declare it (or the timer declares it for you), your Aces are shown to the table and the hand ends.
  11. Series (table option, off by default). If anyone is dealt three of the same number, or three of the same number land on the flushed board, the hand is void: the dealer redeals and the pot stays.
  12. Offline. Once you have opened the app online, it keeps working without a connection for practice against the computer. Hosting, joining, voice and video need a connection.
  13. Rooms. Every open table shows in the Rooms list. Public rooms: tap Join. Private rooms: tap Ask to join and the host lets you in, or join straight away with the code.
  14. Standing rooms. Choose "Keep this room open" when hosting. The room stays with everyone's chips until the host taps "Close room". "Step away" keeps your seat; it shows under Rooms with a Return button.
  15. Friends. Share your friend link once and you're friends. You'll see who's online or at a table, invite them to yours, or join theirs.
  16. Dropped connection. Your seat is held for two minutes (for good in a standing room). Refresh or regain signal and you're back where you were.
  17. Playing with friends. The host opens a table and gets a code. Friends open the same link on any phone or computer and enter the code. The game runs on the server, so the table stays open as long as someone is seated. People who join mid-hand sit in from the next hand.
  18. Voice & video. Mic, camera and sound buttons under your hand. Turn them on to let the table hear and see you; your browser will ask permission once. A seat shows that player's video when their camera is on, a red mic badge when muted, and glows green while they talk.
  19. Timer. On your turn a ring counts down around your avatar. At zero the game acts for you (drops your lowest legal card, or stays). Three timeouts in a row and you're sat out until you tap Rejoin.
  20. Streaks & scores. Win hands in a row and your seat catches fire. Scores & log ranks everyone by what they're up or down since they sat; when the host closes a standing room, everyone sees the final scores.
  21. Chat & treats. Chat holds the Pidgin shouts and a box to say anything to the table. Treats buys drinks and small chops for you, a friend or the whole table. Tap a player to spray them money.
  22. Side bets. Between two players, outside the pot. Ask, they accept, you both agree the amount. Bets on the flushed cards are made before the deal; bets on totals any time before picking. Settled from your own chips while the main game carries on.
Things I assumed, tell me if wrong:
  • Each player gets one picking turn per hand; cards left on the board after the last player are burned.
  • Picks must come from the top of the board in order, never from the middle.
  • Dropped cards don't count toward your 21. If your two kept cards already exceed 21 (Ace + Ace), you're bust for the hand. Lowest possible start is 6 (3 + 3).
  • The "dealer button" rotates each hand to decide who drops first; the house dealer avatar does the physical dealing.
  • If every player is bust, the pot carries over to the next hand.
  • If two or more players hold Adasi, they all win and share the pot equally. A series in a player's hand is shown to everyone when it voids the hand.
  • Side bet "first card higher than second" is settled as soon as the board is flushed; "my total beats yours" at the reveal; equal values are a push (chips returned).
